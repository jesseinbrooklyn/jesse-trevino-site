Parklight

Know when your neighborhood park is busy with other families, and just go. A product concept I scoped from research to a written product spec before writing any code.

Role
Research, product design, spec
Status
Spec written; testing the idea before building
Built so far
A product brief, no app yet

The problem

Parenting young kids can be lonely. You'd take your kid to the park if you knew other families would be there, but you usually don't, so you stay home. The park might be full. You just can't tell.

What the research showed

The apps that exist all work the same way: build a profile, find a compatible family, then schedule a playdate. That's a lot of setup for "is anyone at the park right now?" The gap was a simple signal: heading to the park, who's around?

The core idea

Broadcast, then join, instead of match, then schedule. You see that a park is lively right now, and you go. Parks work like portals: the place is the center of the app, not the people.

Decisions that mattered

  • Kids' safety comes first. No anonymous browsing of nearby families, ever. A list of things the app must never do sits at the top of the spec.
  • Connect only in person. You become friends by scanning each other's QR code at the park, so no stranger can reach you remotely.
  • Score places, not parents. I dropped ideas like ranking active parents. Scoring only parks removed the safety and privacy problems in one move.
  • Invite-only, one neighborhood at a time. The app only works when enough nearby families use it, so it launches dense in one area before moving to the next.
  • Test before building. A neighborhood group chat can test whether parents actually post "heading to the park" before any app exists.

What it shows

This is how I like to start any project: understand the problem, look at what exists, find the one thing that's different, and write down the rules before building. The spec is meant to be argued with, which is the point.
