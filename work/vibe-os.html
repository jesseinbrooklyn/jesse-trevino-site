VIBE.OS

A phone game that teaches "vibe coding," building software by describing what you want to an AI, with real AI characters who write the code, react to your choices and grade the result.

Role

Status

Why
To learn by building something real

The pitch, in five App Store slides

Each slide answers one question, in the order a person swipes through a store listing.

The idea

I taught myself to build with AI, and the hardest part wasn't the code. It was learning how to ask, how to check what came back, and when not to trust it. VIBE.OS turns that into a game. It has a playful Y2K look, and every project you ship goes on a shareable résumé card.

How a round works

  1. Pick a project. The first one is a link-in-bio page, broken into four phases.
  2. Describe what you want. NEXUS, the AI engineer, writes the code, and you see a live preview.
  3. Watch for traps. Some phases plant a misleading request or a likely AI mistake, and you have to catch it.
  4. Get graded. Pixel, the creative director, reacts as you go, and a separate scorer grades the finished build.
  5. Ship it. The finished build gets a grade and goes on your résumé card.

Decisions that mattered

  • Three AI roles, not one. One model writes code, a smaller one plays the character, and a third scores, all running in parallel. Splitting the jobs kept each one simple and fast.
  • Watch the cost. A three-step filter sends easy requests to a simple rule or a small model and only hard ones to the big model, designed to cut AI costs roughly in half.
  • A real brand before more screens. I wrote a character bible, a design spec and a handoff guide first, so AI design tools and people build the same thing.

What I'm learning

How to design around AI that's sometimes wrong, how to keep AI costs predictable, and how to write a brief clear enough that other people, or other AIs, can build from it. Those are the same skills I use on my work tools.
