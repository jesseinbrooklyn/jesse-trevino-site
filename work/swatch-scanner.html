Swatch Scanner

A phone app for showroom staff: point the camera at a drawer of fabric swatches, and it identifies them and builds the reorder, with no spreadsheet.

Role
Product, design and build
Status
Prototype live; ordering not yet connected
Built for
Showroom design staff

The problem

Showrooms keep hundreds of fabric swatches in labeled drawers. When stock runs low, someone fills out a long spreadsheet form by hand: find the material, find the color, find the bin, type the quantity. It's slow, easy to get wrong, and nobody enjoys doing it, so it gets put off.

What I built

A mobile-first web app that reads swatch labels from a photo, matches them against the full swatch list, and turns them into a reorder. It also covers showroom supplies like catalogs and cleaning items, with quantity limits built in. It's designed around how staff already work, so there's almost nothing to learn.

How it works

  1. Pick your showroom and take a photo of a swatch drawer.
  2. Text recognition reads every label in the shot.
  3. A scoring step matches the text to the right material, color and bin, and asks you to choose when there's more than one good match.
  4. Matches go into a reorder list you can review and adjust.
  5. Submit the order.

A problem worth telling

Early versions kept "finding" swatches that weren't there. Words like "Blackout" and "Light Filtering" are printed in the header of every label, and the matcher was reading them as material names. I traced it to the scoring step and added a list of header words that can't count as a match on their own. On the next test photo, all six labels matched with no phantom results.

Project status

Decisions that mattered

  • Works offline. The swatch list ships inside the app instead of loading from a server, because showroom back rooms often have bad signal.
  • Honest about what's unfinished. The final submit step isn't connected to the ordering system yet. Connecting it safely means routing it through a server rather than the phone, and that's the next milestone.
